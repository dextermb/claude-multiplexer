# Language Rule: Write in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE)

Write all copy — chat responses, documents, and artifacts — in ASD-STE100
Simplified Technical English (Issue 9). STE is a controlled language that
makes text clear, unambiguous, and easy to read for all audiences, including
non-native English speakers. Apply these rules by default unless the user
asks for a different style.

STE has two parts: **writing rules** (grammar and style, below) and a
**controlled dictionary** (an approved core vocabulary). Use approved words
where you can, plus permitted *technical nouns* and *technical verbs* for
the subject matter.

## Core principles

- One meaning per word, one word per meaning. Do not use a word to mean
  more than one thing, and do not use several words for the same thing.
- Keep sentences short and direct. Say things in the fewest, plainest words.
- Prefer the active voice and the imperative for instructions.
- Be consistent: use the same term and the same style throughout.

---

## Section 1 — Words

1.1 Use only these kinds of words: words approved in the STE dictionary,
    technical nouns, and technical verbs.
1.2 Use an approved word only as the part of speech it is approved for
    (e.g. if "test" is approved only as a noun, do not use it as a verb).
1.3 Use an approved word only with its approved meaning.
1.4 Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.
1.5 You may use words that fit an approved technical-noun category.
1.6 Use an unapproved word only when it is a technical noun or part of one.
1.7 Do not use a technical noun as a verb.
1.8 Use technical nouns that are accepted in the relevant company, industry,
    or subject field.
1.9 When you must choose a technical noun, choose one that is short and easy
    to understand.
1.10 Do not use regional words, slang, or jargon as technical nouns.
1.11 Do not use different technical nouns for the same item — pick one and
     keep it.
1.12 You may use verbs that fit an approved technical-verb category.
1.13 Do not use a technical verb as a noun.
1.14 Use British English spelling — see [spelling.md](./spelling.md), which
     also says where American spelling stays (identifiers, CSS, Web APIs).

## Section 2 — Multi-word nouns (noun clusters)

2.1 Use multi-word nouns of no more than three words.
2.2 If a technical noun needs more than three words, write it out in full
    (do not compress it into a long noun string). Rephrase to break up
    strings of stacked modifiers.

## Section 3 — Verbs

3.1 Use only the verb forms given in the dictionary.
3.2 Use only these verb forms and tenses:
    - infinitive (base) form
    - imperative (command) form
    - simple present tense
    - simple past tense
    - simple future tense
    - past participle (used only as an adjective).
    Do NOT use: present perfect, past perfect, present/past progressive
    (-ing continuous), or any other complex/compound verb construction.
3.3 Use the past participle only as an adjective (e.g. "the adjusted valve").
3.4 Do not use auxiliary/helping verbs to build complex verb constructions.
3.5 Use the "-ing" form of a verb only as a technical noun or as a modifier
    inside a technical name — never as a continuous-tense verb.
3.6 Use the active voice. In descriptive writing, use the passive voice only
    when it is unavoidable; in procedures, always use the active voice.
3.7 Describe an action with an approved verb, not with a noun or another
    part of speech (say "Connect the hose", not "Make a connection").

## Section 4 — Sentences

4.1 Write short, clear sentences.
4.2 Do not omit words or use contractions to shorten sentences. Keep
    grammatical words (articles, "that", etc.); write "do not", not "don't".
4.3 Use a vertical (bulleted or numbered) list to present complex material.
4.4 Use connecting words and phrases (e.g. "and", "but", "then", "because",
    "to do this") to link related sentences and show their relationship.
4.5 Where applicable, use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative
    adjective (this, these) before a noun for clarity.

## Section 5 — Procedural writing (instructions)

5.1 Write short sentences: **maximum 20 words** per sentence in a procedure.
5.2 Write only one instruction per sentence — unless two or more actions
    happen at the same time.
5.3 Write instructions in the imperative (command) form ("Open the valve").
5.4 When a condition must be known first, put the condition at the START of
    the sentence, before the command ("If the lamp is on, close the switch").
5.5 Write notes to give information only — never put an instruction in a note.

## Section 6 — Descriptive writing

6.1 Give information gradually, in a logical order.
6.2 Use key words and key phrases to give the text a clear structure.
6.3 Write short sentences: **maximum 25 words** per sentence in descriptive
    text.
6.4 Use paragraphs to group related information.
6.5 Give each paragraph only one topic.
6.6 Keep each paragraph to no more than six sentences.

## Section 7 — Safety instructions / warnings

7.1 Use a signal word (e.g. "Warning", "Caution") to show the level of risk.
7.2 Start a safety instruction with a clear, accurate command or condition.
7.3 Add an explanation that states the risk or the possible result.

## Section 8 — Punctuation and word count

8.1 You may use all standard English punctuation EXCEPT the semicolon (;).
8.2 Use hyphens (-) to join words that are directly related.
8.3 You may use parentheses to: refer to illustrations or text; give letters
    or numbers that identify items; identify work steps; give abbreviations;
    show singular and plural of a noun together; explain words or part of a
    sentence; or give an alternative.
8.4 In a vertical list, a colon (:) counts the same as a period for word
    count and marks the end of a sentence.
8.5 Text inside parentheses counts as one word in the sentence.
8.6 Count each of these as ONE word: numbers; numbers with units;
    abbreviations; alphanumeric identifiers; quoted text; titles, headings,
    placards, and labels; and proper nouns of people, groups, organizations,
    and geopolitical entities.
8.7 A hyphenated word counts as one word.

## Section 9 — Writing practices

9.1 If a strict word-for-word rewrite would produce awkward or unclear text,
    change the sentence construction instead of forcing it.
9.2 Use each approved word correctly and in line with its dictionary entry.
9.3 Do not combine two words into a phrasal verb (say "start", not "start up";
    "continue", not "carry on").
9.4 Choose terminology and wording with a consistent style, and keep it
    consistent throughout the whole text.

---

## Quick checklist before sending

- [ ] Short sentences (≤20 words procedures / ≤25 words descriptions).
- [ ] One instruction per sentence; imperative for commands.
- [ ] Active voice; simple tenses only (no perfect / progressive).
- [ ] No contractions, no omitted grammar words.
- [ ] No semicolons; no phrasal verbs; no slang or jargon.
- [ ] One term per concept, used consistently.
- [ ] Conditions placed before commands.
- [ ] Warnings: signal word + command + reason.
- [ ] Paragraphs: one topic, ≤6 sentences.
